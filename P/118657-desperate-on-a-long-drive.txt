Desperate on a Long Drive

Author: pee98754

*000*2025-12-05 19:08

Longtime reader, first-time poster, and I finally had an experience this week worth sharing.

For context: I’m a woman in my early 30s, 5'11", curvy, long brunette hair, glasses. Earlier this week I had to drive about 250 miles for work, and I’d taken the morning off to recover from a rough night’s sleep before hitting the road in the afternoon.

I woke up around 9:30 with a pounding headache—usually dehydration—so I tried to flush it out before leaving. I drank about 1.5 L of water plus a 500 ml energy drink for the caffeine, assuming I’d have plenty of time to get it out of my system before driving. Three hours later I had what I thought would be my final bathroom stop, packed my things, and headed out.

I’ve done this drive before and usually stop at the services halfway. There’s not much else on that stretch of motorway, and the place before it is grim, so the plan was set.

I left around 2pm, stopped for a big Starbucks 30 minutes in because I was still exhausted, and drank that plus some water as I drove. About an hour and a half later I felt the first heaviness in my bladder—nothing alarming yet—so I just shifted a little and kept going.

But thirty, forty minutes later, that heaviness had become a proper urge. I passed the exit for the last services before my target (another 20–25 minutes to go), and of course that’s when the rain started hammering down—big, loud drops that made everything ten times worse. My podcast kept cutting out too, leaving me alone with my thoughts and a very full bladder. I was bouncing my leg without even noticing.

With about fifteen minutes left, I finally undid the button on my jeans and eased the seatbelt away from my stomach. The relief was instant but temporary. My hand drifted between my legs, pressing down hard, because every minute felt like a battle at that point.

Two miles from the exit, traffic suddenly slowed. My bladder felt solid. I caught myself holding my breath with every wave of pressure. I honestly wasn’t sure I could make it.

Seeing the exit sign felt like being saved.

The car park was nearly empty, so I headed for a space right by the entrance. It was getting dark, and as I got out to grab my bag the cold air hit me so sharply I had to stop, cross my legs, and squeeze to get control back.

Then I noticed the faint yellow lines. I’d parked in a restricted space.

I had two choices: run inside immediately and get the relief I was seconds from losing… or get back in and move the car, risking a ticket if I ignored the markings. And this particular services has a terrible reputation for fines.

Swearing under my breath, I forced myself back into the car, moving like I was made of glass. Reversed. Parked again. Got out. Somehow still dry.

I walked as fast as I dared into the building, around to the newly refurbished toilets, and very nearly walked into the men’s by mistake. The moment I found the women’s, I bolted into the first free stall.

The sound inside was unreal—women absolutely gushing in the other cubicles, the kind of desperate service-station soundtrack you only hear on long journeys. I fumbled the lock, twisted around, crossed my legs tight while yanking down my jeans and thong, and I was already leaking by the time I hit the seat.

Then everything just let go.

I must have peed solidly for nearly a minute—loud, hissing, relentless. The relief was honestly incredible.

Afterwards I went to get some food, but instead of eating in the car like usual, I took a table inside, knowing full well I’d refill again quickly—and I did. Once I'd wee'd again, I finished the drive without any more drama.

I have to make the return journey tomorrow… so fingers crossed I don’t repeat the experience.
